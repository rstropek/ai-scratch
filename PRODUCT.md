# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

Scope: the KI-Scratch app and the video page for the event (`/digiferien-2026-live`, still online). The slide deck has its own record in `slides/PRODUCT.md`.

## Platform

web

## Users

- **Kids aged 10–16** (about 200 at 10 Austrian schools), coding beginners who know a little Scratch. They work in the app: the Scratch 3 editor with a German AI helper beside it.
- **Teachers** who run the day at their school, without a live trainer.

## Product Purpose

A one-day AI workshop, "Digitalferien 2026", run once next week. Kids follow Rainer's video tutorial and build their own Scratch games with the AI helper. Success: the day runs without hiccups at every school.

## Operating Context

- The video tutorial (seven Descript videos, about 2.5 hours) leads the day; the teachers start it and the videos explain everything else.
- Kids save their games in the CoderDojo Locker with the code on a Locker card their teacher hands out.
- Desktop browsers at schools.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- German UI; desktop only.
- The code is published under AGPL: codes, keys and school data never go into the repo or the public bundle (the video list is public).
- Kids' KI access codes: one per school, not case-sensitive.

## Brand Commitments

- Event name: "Digitalferien 2026" (the repo says Digiferien).
- The app's look is Scratch 3's own GUI palette (`src/client/index.css`); the video page uses it too (Rainer's choice). The slides use a separate sticker-album look.
- Text on the video page addresses no one directly ("Schule wählen", "Video starten"): kids may read along on the projector.

## Evidence on Hand

- No photos, testimonials or numbers about the event exist; none may be invented.

## Product Principles

- Reliable on the day beats feature-rich.
- Teachers are not tech experts: every step obvious, nothing to configure.
- Text for kids and teachers is German; code is English.
